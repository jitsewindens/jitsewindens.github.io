For my third Tech&Meet, I attended "Protecting Belgium: Jobs in Cybersecurity". 
Given everything that's happening in the world right now with cyber attacks and 
phishing, this topic felt really relevant. On top of that, I was 
genuinely curious about what Belgium actually has to offer in terms of 
cybersecurity jobs and this Tech&Meet delivered on both fronts.

## The speakers and organisations

Several organisations were represented during this session, each with their 
own role in Belgium's cyber landscape:

- **CCB (Centre for Cybersecurity Belgium)**: the national authority for 
cybersecurity, focused on preventing, detecting and stopping attacks. They 
also work closely with CERT, which handles incident response.
- **Federal Police**: responsible for cyber law enforcement: investigating 
and prosecuting cybercrime.
- **ADIV/SGRS**: the Belgian military intelligence service, handling cyber 
defence and offensive operations for the military.
- **Cyber Command**: with missions ranging from intelligence (001) to active 
cyber operations.
- **eGov**: a non-profit set up by the federal government in 2001, whose goal 
is to help staff IT services across 40+ federal organisations.

What was interesting is that the CCB took time to explain what overlaps and 
what differs between their work and that of military organisations like ADIV. 
They work for the entire country but operate with clear priorities depending 
on the threat level.

## What I learned

One of the most eye-opening parts was seeing how many different career paths 
exist within cybersecurity in Belgium alone. Whether you're interested in 
intelligence, law enforcement, policy or technical defence, there's a role 
for you somewhere in this ecosystem.

The talk also touched on combat aircraft and drone technology, which I find 
absolutely fascinating. Seeing how cyber operations connect to physical 
military assets like these made the whole picture much more concrete.

The concept of **cyber intelligence sharing**: collecting, evaluating and 
informing across organisations also stood out to me as something that's 
often overlooked but absolutely critical to making the whole system work.

## My reflection

This was another really strong Tech&Meet for me. Cybersecurity is a topic I 
care about, and seeing how Belgium organises its defences across civilian, 
police and military structures was genuinely fascinating. It also confirmed 
that this is a field I want to explore further when thinking about my career.

The combination of technical content and career insights made this one of the 
more practical sessions I've attended. I'd recommend it to anyone who is even 
slightly curious about working in cybersecurity in Belgium.
