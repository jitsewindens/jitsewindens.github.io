In this blog, I'll take you through my day at the Hack The Future hackathon 
in Antwerp. I must admit that I wasn't really looking forward to it before I 
arrived, but that changed quite quickly thanks to the warm and professional 
welcome from the organisers.

<img width="1080"  height=auto alt="1000007103" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/f11f7e24-5e6e-4449-bfcb-2b7cd21c8b0c" />

## Morning

First, there was a presentation for everyone explaining how the hackathon 
worked and which teams everyone had been assigned to. After that, we were led 
to the right room with our team (where I didn't know anyone). I had signed up 
for the 'Open Ocean with AI by unlockd!' challenge; I'd done this because I 
want to learn as much as possible about AI, as I believe it really is the 
future.

After a full explanation and input session from the lecturers at unlockd, we 
were split into teams of two. I was paired with Nikolaos, who, like me, was 
studying applied computer science. We immediately got to work on all the 
necessary configurations and set-up before we could really start on the task 
itself.

## The task

The aim was to develop a weather app using Flutter and Gemini AI. We created 
a user interface where you could ask a weather-related question, to which 
Gemini would provide an appropriate answer and a corresponding graph. On the 
face of it, this seems simple, but making that connection between Flutter, 
Gemini and your own user interface is a lot harder than we thought. We got 
stuck for ages just trying to get anything on the screen at all, but once 
we'd got the hang of it, it was a matter of steering Gemini in the right 
direction and receiving the correct answers.

## Lunch

We were really put in the spotlight here, as we were treated to sandwiches 
and a drink at lunch. This gave us a chance to do some networking and get to 
know new people. We even took a quick stroll through the zoo, to which we 
had free entry that day.

## The finale

After lunch, we still had to get the graphs to display correctly using Gemini 
and make the site look a bit nicer. We mainly had problems getting the graphs 
to display correctly, but we managed that in the end too. As a grand finale, 
everyone had to pitch their site to the rest of the room, and the two best 
were invited to give a speech in the main hall for the grand prize. 
Unfortunately, we weren't there for that. We also got to enjoy some snacks 
and drinks at the end.

## My reflection

I'm actually quite surprised that I enjoyed this so much. I went in with a 
very critical eye and came home feeling positive. Everything was absolutely 
spot on, from the organisation to the people taking part. It's definitely 
worth doing again!
