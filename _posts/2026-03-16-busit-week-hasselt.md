In the last week of April 2026, I had the opportunity to take part in the 
International BusIT Week, hosted by PXL-Digital in Hasselt. Our Howest 
delegation consisted of four students: Hryhorii Petrenko, Michiel Van Mulders, 
Wannes Vantorre and myself. Over the course of five days, we worked alongside 
students from Cardiff Metropolitan, Hanze, EPHEC and AP, a truly international 
mix that made the experience even more valuable and interesting.

## Day 1: Kickoff & ROS2 introduction

After arriving at the PXL campus at 9:00 AM, we were welcomed by our two 
lecturers, Sam van Rijn and Tim Dupont. The day started with an introduction 
to Robotics and ROS2, covering the history of robotics and how it's used in 
practice today. One robot that stood out was the SpotMini from Boston Dynamics,
a compact, fully electric robot dog first unveiled in 2017, which eventually 
evolved into the commercial Spot used today for complex inspections.

After lunch we dove into ROS basics and more advanced techniques, finishing 
the day with our first Gazebo bots workshop where we experimented with making 
a virtual car drive around a circuit using keyboard controls.

## Day 2: Computer Vision

<img width="945" height="594" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/dc5fac66-1c3a-4568-a248-dc952d8ed4e2" />

Day two focused entirely on computer vision. Our challenge was to use computer 
vision to get a car to autonomously complete a lap of the circuit, recognising 
the track and correcting its course whenever it went off. We used colour 
masks to identify the yellow lines on both edges of the track and keep the car 
within them. It took a lot of trial and error to get the colour detection right, 
but once it worked, we could even push the speed up. A very fascinating exercise.

## Day 3: Idea pitching & cultural activity

On day three we each pitched three project ideas to the professors. The winning 
concept, chosen by the lecturers, was a warehouse robot whose job was to look for 
missing pallets. After spending an hour mapping out the key features and 
dividing responsibilities within our team, we headed out for a cultural 
activity.

We visited Hophemel, a craft beer store in Hasselt where the owner gave us a 
behind-the-scenes tour of their brewing equipment.

## Day 4: Project development & farewell dinner

The fourth day was fully dedicated to building our project. Everyone 
contributed their own strengths and the intensive teamwork not only moved the 
project forward but also improved how we communicated as a group. In the 
evening we attended the farewell dinner, a relaxed and memorable way to 
celebrate everything we had built and the connections we had made during 
the week.

## Day 5: Final presentations

On the last day we polished up our project and prepared our presentation. 
Each group then presented their work in front of the class. Although it was 
nerve-racking, it was also a great moment to show what we had accomplished.

## My reflection

This BusIT week was one of the most fun learning experiences I've had 
during my studies. In just five days I went from knowing very little about 
ROS2 and computer vision to actually building a working simulation and 
presenting a full project concept. Working in an international team pushed 
me to communicate more clearly and think more creatively.

The combination of technical workshops, an international environment and 
cultural activities made this week truly unique. I would absolutely recommend 
this to any IT student who gets the chance to participate.
