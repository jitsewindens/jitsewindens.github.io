My second Tech&Meet is a fact! I attended "The Birth of NATO's Cyber 
Defence", who are settled at the NATO headquarters in Brussels. As someone who is 
genuinely interested in NATO and military technology, this was right up my 
alley and it didn't disappoint.

## The speaker

The speaker was a civil engineer with a military background, working out of 
the NATO office in Brussels. He had hands-on experience with topics like air 
traffic control, F16 operations and cyber defence strategy, which immediately 
gave the talk a lot of credibility.

## What was covered

The talk kicked off with a historical example: the 
2010 Iranian nuclear program attack, better known as Stuxnet. This was malware 
that managed to physically damage nuclear centrifuges in Iran, showing that 
cyber attacks can have very real, physical consequences. It was a wake-up call 
for many nations, including NATO's 31 allied members.

A big theme throughout the talk was that cyber defence is still heavily 
underestimated. Things like contingency plans and disaster recovery plans are 
often deprioritized, even though they are critical. They made us very clear that
if it's not cyber safe, there is no mission.

Another concept that stuck with me was **hybrid warfare**, where you attack 
one target but cause effects somewhere completely different. A striking example 
was North Korean hacking groups using AI to apply for jobs at Western tech 
companies, essentially getting insider access to infiltrate entire 
organizations. This kind of attack is hard to detect and even harder to defend 
against.

We also discussed **critical national infrastructure**, which is much broader 
than most people think. It's not just power plants and bridges, but also mobile 
networks and everyday digital systems. The 2024 NATO Integrated Cyber Defence 
System was presented as a step towards better protecting all this.

On the regulatory side, the speaker brought on several key European laws: the 
NIS2 directive, the Cyber Resilience Act and the Cyber Solidarity Act. These 
are pushing organizations to take security seriously at a structural level.

## My reflection

This was by one of the most interesting talk sessions I've attended. 
The combination of military context, real-world examples and current regulations 
made everything feel very relevant.

The speaker emphasized that proper use of IT and recognising phishing attacks should start at a young age. 
That's something I completely agree with. Cyber defence isn't just a government 
or military problem, it starts with every individual user.

I would absolutely recommend this kind of event to anyone interested in cybersecurity or Nato
